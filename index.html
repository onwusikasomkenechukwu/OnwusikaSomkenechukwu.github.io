Primary Research

ppg-pipeline: Utility-Aligned Quality Gating for Wrist-PPG Heart-Rate Estimation

Co-authored with Dr. Saurav Aryal at Howard University. The manuscript is submitted to IEEE TBME.

Should wrist-PPG quality be judged by waveform shape or by downstream heart-rate utility? The answer overturns a convention inherited from finger pulse oximetry: skewness-based gating, the dominant practice, actively harms heart-rate estimation under motion.

  • 3.70 bpm error from the utility gate versus roughly 14 bpm under skewness gating, replicated across 3 datasets and 4 estimator families, p<0.001.
  • Validated with subject-wise LOSO and LODO splits, bootstrap confidence intervals, and McNemar gate-disagreement analysis.
  • A gate trained only on FFT labels cut a sparse-reconstruction estimator's error in half on held-out datasets, with no retraining.
